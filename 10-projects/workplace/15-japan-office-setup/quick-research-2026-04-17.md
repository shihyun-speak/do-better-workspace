## Part 1 — Hiring a Freelancer for the Office Move/Setup

### Framing

We need **a local person (freelancer or consultant) to help execute the Japan office setup** — coordinating the physical move/fit-out, liaising with vendors (coworking operators, IT, furniture, utilities, telco), handling on-the-ground tasks we can't do remotely, and bridging JP-EN where needed. This is different from "post a generic admin task online" — we need someone with **project-management chops + bilingual capability + ideally prior office-setup experience** in Tokyo.

Three sourcing paths, ordered by fit:

1. **Office relocation specialists** (best for turnkey, but pricey and more "agency" than "freelancer")
2. **Bilingual freelancer platforms** (best for finding a PM-type individual at reasonable cost)
3. **Local service / gig platforms** (for piecemeal tasks — cleaning, furniture assembly, errands)

### Detailed profiles — office-move specialists

**SEIKA Relocation Japan** — Corporate relocation, tenancy management, immigration support for businesses moving to Tokyo. Best-fit if we want a single vendor to handle the full move. Pricing on inquiry **[confirm]**.

**Japan Mobility** — International team of relocation professionals; destination services (apartment/office hunt, visa, utilities, moving coordination, cultural adjustment). Positioned for expat-heavy setups.

**All Japan Relocation** — Covers Tokyo, Yokohama, Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe. Standard relocation vendor — good alternative quote vs. SEIKA.

**Relo Japan** — Offices in Tokyo + Nagoya; "one-stop" for business relocations. Deep enterprise client base.

**Startup Tokyo Partners** — Specifically for **foreign-entrepreneur setup in Tokyo**: business consultants + legal specialists, bank account opening, company setup, Business Manager visas, office permits. If we don't yet have a registered JP entity or need help on the legal/corporate side in parallel with the office setup, this is the right door.

### Detailed profiles — freelancer platforms for a bilingual PM

**Workshift** — Built for JP ↔ global freelancer matching. Bilingual UI, escrow payments. Strong categories: Business Services, Translation & Interpreting, Market Research. **Best for us:** post a bilingual "office setup project manager" contract — likely the single best platform for this specific need.

**Upwork (Tokyo filter)** — Lists 11+ freelance project managers in Tokyo with bilingual JP/EN skills and experience bridging JP clients + international teams. Faster sourcing than agencies; global payment infra we likely already use. **Best for us:** finding a freelance PM with a portable contract.

**Zehitomo** — JP's Thumbtack-equivalent: matches local businesses/freelancers (cleaning, photographers, handyman, moving help, etc.) with clients. **Best for us:** piecemeal on-the-ground services during the move (office cleaning, furniture assembly, errand runs). Not where we find our PM, but useful once a PM is coordinating vendors.

**CrowdWorks / Lancers / coconala** — Japanese domestic crowdsourcing. Huge talent pool but **Japanese-only UI** makes them impractical for us to run directly. Mention here so we know what they are, but skip unless our PM wants to tap them for subcontracted JP-language work.

**Pasona** — Japan's largest staffing/BPO group; explicit foreign-affiliated company practice (office setup, handbook drafting, HR consulting, contract staffing). **Best for:** handing off admin/ops build-out as a package if we don't want to freelance-wrangle.

### Recommended first moves

1. **Post on Workshift + browse Upwork Tokyo PMs** — goal: identify 2-3 bilingual freelance PMs with prior office-setup experience in Tokyo. Fastest and cheapest path to having a local quarterback.
2. **Request quotes from SEIKA + one other relocation agency** (Japan Mobility or Relo Japan) — in parallel. If the agency package is within budget and saves speed, it may be worth the premium over a freelance PM.
3. **Ping Startup Tokyo Partners** only if we still have open questions on JP entity/legal/visa work alongside the office setup (they bundle it).
4. **Keep Zehitomo in pocket** for discrete on-the-ground tasks once our PM is in place.

## Part 2 — Coworking Shortlist (Meguro + Shibuya)

### Landscape (3-line summary)

- Shibuya has the **densest cluster** of premium and English-friendly coworking in Tokyo — WeWork (multiple buildings), Regus, Business-Airport (Tokyu), and a wave of newer spaces around Shibuya Sakura Stage (opened 2023–24).
- Meguro is **quieter and cheaper**, mostly boutique / design-forward (MIDORI.so Nakameguro, Impact HUB) with one premium option at Meguro Arco Tower (The Executive Centre).
- For a ~30-person team, the realistic shortlist is **WeWork (Shibuya Scramble, Iceberg), The Executive Centre Meguro, Regus Shibuya/Nakameguro, Business-Airport Shibuya Sakura Stage, CROSSCOOP Shibuya** — all offer team-size private offices with some English support.
